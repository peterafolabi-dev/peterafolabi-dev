02 / The flat map

The world, unrolled.

The same planet drawn as a flat equirectangular map, one pixel at a time on the GPU. Switch between relief, biomes, temperature and rainfall. Hover for coordinates; click to drop the probe, and the globe turns to face it.

Hover the map · click to probe

Temperature is a simple model (about 29 °C at the equator falling with latitude and 6.5 °C per kilometre of height). Rainfall is the world’s generated moisture field, not real climate data. Move the sea level or generate a new world and this map redraws.

04 / Day length

Hours of light.

How long the Sun stays up at any latitude through the year. The dot shows today’s date on the Sun and seasons slider above. Pick a latitude and see sunrise, sunset and the noon height of the Sun.

Idealised: sunrise and sunset are the moments the Sun’s centre is on the horizon, in local solar time, with no atmospheric refraction.

06 / Reshape

The seed is a point in noise space. Move it and the continents morph into new ones, smoothly. Every probe reading, zone and fact above adapts to the new geography.

07 / Winds

Belts of
wind.

Uneven heating and the planet’s spin sort the air into three circulation cells in each hemisphere. Near the equator the trade winds blow from the east; in mid-latitudes the westerlies blow from the west; near the poles the winds turn easterly again. The streaks on the globe follow those belts.

Trade winds 0–30°Westerlies 30–60°Polar easterlies 60–90°

Where the trade winds meet near the equator the air rises, forming the rain belt called the ITCZ. It follows the overhead Sun north and south through the year, which is why many tropical regions have a wet and a dry season.

08 / Measure

Distance
on a sphere.

Click two points on the globe. The shortest path between them is a great circle, which is why flights curve over the poles on flat maps. Or load a preset pair of coordinates.

Scroll here to switch the globe to measure mode.

The world is generated, so only the coordinates of the presets come from real cities. Distances use Earth’s mean radius of 6,371 km.

09 / Inside the planet

Cut it
open.

A planet is layers. Choose one to highlight it. Thicknesses and temperatures are Earth's (approximate); the crust is exaggerated so you can see it.

10 / The atmosphere

Up through
the air.

Drag the slider from sea level to 10,000 km. The scale is logarithmic, so the thin layers near the ground get as much room as the vast ones near space.

Temperature and pressure follow the US Standard Atmosphere up to 100 km (approximate). Above that, temperature depends on solar activity.

Longitude and time

14 / Plate tectonics

A cracked
shell.

Earth’s crust and uppermost mantle are broken into plates that drift a few centimetres a year, about as fast as fingernails grow. Where they meet you find mountains, trenches, earthquakes and volcanoes.

The seven major plates area · typical speed (approx.)

Three kinds of boundary

About 90% of earthquakes and roughly three-quarters of active volcanoes lie along the Pacific “Ring of Fire”.

17 / World clocks

Right now.

The live time in eighteen cities, from your device’s clock and the browser’s time-zone database. Noon is not the same moment everywhere because the planet turns 15° of longitude per hour.

18 / Deep time

Earth’s history in
one day.

Squash 4.54 billion years into 24 hours, starting at midnight when Earth forms. Almost nothing familiar happens until the final minutes.

Dates are rounded; scientists revise them as new evidence appears.
